Design thesis.

Two arguments
to defend.

Brief asks for two defenses: the mobile pattern, and the scrolled treatment.

— Para 1 · Mobile pattern: hamburger over bottom-sheet

I chose hamburger drawer over a bottom sheet. The bottom-sheet pattern (Wolt, Yad2) is a marketplace tell — it implies a persistent action surface that lives at the thumb. A magazine doesn't carry that posture; the reader sets the cadence. Mehamakor's mobile audience opens the app to read a producer's story, not to flip through 200 listings — so the chrome can sit quiet at the top and step out of the way. The hamburger is a single 44×44 target at the start corner (right in RTL), and the drawer it opens is just three links and the two CTAs — Frank Ruhl 700 at 24px, with the editorial numeral 01·02·03 on the end of each row carrying the same gold accent the category grid established. Same magazine vocabulary, smaller binding.

— Para 2 · Scrolled treatment: cream pill, single hairline shadow

The default state is transparent — nav letters sit directly on the cream canvas, indistinguishable from running editorial type, no chrome. Once scroll passes the threshold, the navbar resolves into a floating pill: --bg-card fill, 1px --border outline, the single allowed scroll-header shadow at 6% opacity. One token, one shape, one transition — 280ms ease on background/border/padding. No backdrop blur on the page itself (Park: glass is an ingredient, not the meal). The pill says: "I noticed you've started reading. I'll wait here so you can find your way back." A marketplace navbar would lock to the top and assert; a magazine navbar floats, holds, and yields.
